About this guide

I watch a lot of Premier League football, and the grounds are a big part of why. A match at Craven Cottage looks nothing like a match at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, even on TV. Some of these places have been in use since the 1800s, and some opened last year.

This site puts all twenty in one place with the basics for each: who plays there, where it is, how many it holds and when it opened. Capacities are the figures listed for the start of the 2026-27 season. They change most years as clubs add or rebuild stands.

Questions

The bottom three clubs are relegated each year and three come up from the Championship, so three grounds swap out. For 2026-27, Coventry City, Ipswich Town and Hull City came up.

Clubs report a licensed capacity to the league each season, and it moves when seats are added, removed or blocked off for building work. Older articles often quote a figure from a previous season.

Stamford Bridge opened in 1877 as an athletics ground, before Chelsea existed. Anfield and Portman Road both date from 1884.

Clubs sell naming rights to sponsors, which is why names like Emirates, Etihad, Gtech and Vitality appear. A lot of fans still use the older name, such as Dean Court for Bournemouth's ground.
